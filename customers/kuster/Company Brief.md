## Company background
Küster (founded 1926) is a global Tier-N automotive supplier with 3,000+ employees across 16 companies on 4 continents (Germany, Slovakia, USA, Mexico, Brazil, China), specializing in electromechanical actuation/adjustment systems, actuators, and drive cables. The company positions itself around CO2 reduction, safety, and comfort. Its core market is shifting fast: the automotive actuator market is moving toward electric actuation and software-defined-vehicle motion control, with the segment valued at ~$16.1B in 2025 (source: MarketsandMarkets / openPR), pulling suppliers like Küster toward smarter, sensor-rich, data-driven components.

## Edge Impulse relationship
Per CRM (10/14 note): one call post-kickoff, with a second scheduled. The account is genuinely early — they are starting over from scratch after a prior trial that used a different model and old data. They are now waiting on a complete new dataset tied to a new project/machine part before they can meaningfully progress. What's working: engagement is live and there's a defined next project. What's stuck: everything is blocked on data readiness, which is the classic onboarding stall point.

## Retention assessment
Renewal is 2028-09-01 — the longest runway in the portfolio — so there is no near-term contractual risk. Health is Neutral (65) and risk is Average (5). The real risk here is not churn timing but momentum: an account stuck in Onboarding/Ideation, restarting after a failed trial, with no assigned CSM, can quietly stall and reach a usage of zero long before renewal. The data dependency is the single point of failure — if the new dataset slips, the project dies on the vine.

## Expansion opportunities
Küster's products are physically ideal for edge ML: actuators and drive systems are prime candidates for end-of-line quality inspection (vibration/acoustic anomaly detection) and in-field predictive maintenance. Angles: (1) anchor the current new-part project as a manufacturing QC/defect-detection use case; (2) extend to predictive maintenance on actuation systems aligned with the SDV/electric-actuation trend; (3) once one plant proves value, replicate across the 16-company footprint.

## Recommended next actions
1. Assign a CSM — this account currently has none and is in the most fragile (early) stage.
2. Hold the scheduled second call; get a firm date for the new dataset and define success criteria for the new model.
3. Provide data-collection guidance/templates to de-risk the dataset dependency (the main blocker).
4. Reframe the project explicitly as a QC or predictive-maintenance use case with a measurable outcome.
5. Schedule a 30/60-day progress checkpoint to prevent a silent onboarding stall.
